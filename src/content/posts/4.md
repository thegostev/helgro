<div class="prose-col">
<div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 02 · Śródmieście</div>
<h2>Streets that confuse people</h2></div>

<p>Between the café and the water we're observing streets. Sometimes people from Eastern Europe see Gdynia and think socialist housing blocks - that's true to some minor stylistic part. Downtown went up 1933–1937, on a strict grid, before Soviet architecture existed at all. Modernism was big in Europe in the 30s, and Gdynia's center survived as one coherent piece, which is why it sits on UNESCO's tentative list today.</p>

<div class="gt-stat-row" aria-label="Gdynia by the numbers: a fishing village of 28 houses; over one hundred thousand residents within a decade; about two hundred thousand people living in a city planned for a million">
<div class="gt-stat">
<div class="n">~28</div>
<div class="l">The fishing village of 28 houses</div>
<div class="d">Transformed after WWI as Gdańsk became a Free City run mostly by Germans, and Poland lost practical access to the sea. So it built a new port from this tiny village.</div>
</div>
<div class="gt-stat">
<div class="n">100K+</div>
<div class="l">Residents within about a decade</div>
<div class="d">Plots were sold to private developers with hard rules about facades. Everything proportional and though through.</div>
</div>
<div class="gt-stat">
<div class="n">200K+</div>
<div class="l">Living in a city sized for a million</div>
<div class="d">The extra space feels good. A city architect made private greed produce a coherent city.</div>
</div>
</div>

<p>Gdynia feels like something built for a million people, but around 200,000 live here.</p>

<section class="chapter" id="blyskawica">
<div class="prose-col">
<div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 05 · ORP Błyskawica</div>
<h2>From lounging to war in one dock</h2></div>

<p>ORP Błyskawica "Lightning" was built in Cowes, England, in 1936–1937 for the Polish Navy, and got out when the war started. Her best-known act came full circle: in May 1942 she helped defend Cowes itself from Luftwaffe raids, firing on German bombers over the town where she was born.</p>
</div>

<figure class="bigphoto">
<div class="photo"><img src="/images/blog/lawrence-gdynia-mast.jpg" alt="The mast of ORP Błyskawica — antenna rings, signal lines and a red-and-white flag against a gray sky" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption>Look closely at the mast shot. Antenna rings, signal lines, a red-and-white flag against blue-grayish sky. The ship is preserved down to the wires.</figcaption>
</figure>

<figure class="bigphoto">
<div class="photo"><img src="/images/blog/lawrence-gdynia-bow.jpg" alt="Lawrence and Alex in front of the bow of ORP Błyskawica, riveted steel rising overhead" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption>This bow shot gives you the scale that photos of whole warships never do. Riveted steel rising a few stories over your head. Błyskawica is the most decorated Polish warship and one of the few WWII destroyers still afloat.</figcaption>
</figure>
</section>
