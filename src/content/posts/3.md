<section class="chapter" id="oldtown">
  <div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 01 · Gdańsk</div>
    <h2>Old town, where the city ends</h2></div>

  <p>We started near the old granaries, and I gave Jonas the thing tourist guides never tell — mostly because <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_Hrytsak" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Yaroslav Hrytsak</a> first stitched it together. When Columbus opened the Americas, silver flooded Europe and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_revolution" target="_blank" rel="noopener">prices went insane</a>. Grain became the new gold. Ukrainian grain moved up through Gdańsk and out across the Baltic, dodging the Arab-controlled Mediterranean entirely. That trade built these warehouses. It's directly connected to the creation of the first modern country that would eventually become Ukraine — the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Cossack Hetmanate</a>.</p>

  <p>We stopped at the monument to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Hevelius" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jan Heweliusz</a>. Dutch-rooted astronomer, the center of this city's story.</p>

  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Johannes Hevelius"></div>

  <p>Here's the part I like: Gdańsk's gorgeous center sits on the <em>edge</em> of the city. Step past it and it's just fields. The lone tower left from the Teutonic Knights' castle tells you why — when Poland finally beat the order whose taxes had long squeezed the city, people tore the castle down brick by brick and built their own houses out of it.</p>

  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Gdańsk Castle"></div>
  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Teutonic Order"></div>

  <p>We cut through Mariacka Street — amber stalls down both sides, the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Church,_Gda%C5%84sk" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Gothic church</a> and its green spires hanging over everything — and stopped a while to take it in. On the way to the shipyard, a spring market under white tents, blue-and-white ribbons snapping in the wind, families packed onto long checkered tables.</p>

<section class="chapter" id="shipyard">
  <div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 02 · Stocznia Gdańska</div>
    <h2>The shipyard, and a lesson about language</h2></div>

  <p>If the old town is the city's postcard, the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk_Shipyard" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Stocznia</a> is its real turning point — birthplace of Polish democracy and the whole Eastern European push against Soviet rule. At its peak it ran like a small city of its own.</p>

  <div class="stats">
    <div class="stat"><div class="n">17,000</div><div class="l">workers at peak — with its own depot, cinema and radio station</div></div>
    <div class="stat"><div class="n">1 / 2&nbsp;wks</div><div class="l">a new ship finished roughly every two weeks</div></div>
    <div class="stat"><div class="n">10M</div><div class="l">Solidarity members at its height — a loophole that swallowed a regime</div></div>
  </div>

  <figure><div class="photo"><img src="/images/blog/IMG_8609.jpeg" alt="The shipyard gates — birthplace of Polish democracy." loading="lazy"></div>
    <figcaption>The shipyard gates — birthplace of Polish democracy.</figcaption></figure>

  <p>I told Jonas about <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Wa%C5%82%C4%99sa" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Lech Wałęsa</a>. Blue-collar electrician, no connections — which is exactly why people trusted him. Here's the kicker: he wasn't even the one who started the uprising. He was just the one who could <em>speak</em> — better than the others. He became the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_(Polish_trade_union)" target="_blank" rel="noopener">symbol of the revolution</a>, won the <a href="https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1983/walesa/facts/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Nobel Peace Prize</a> in 1983, and was elected <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Polish_presidential_election" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Poland's first democratically elected president</a> in 1990.</p>

  <div class="pull">"So what do we learn from history? Learn language. Speak properly."
    <cite>— to Jonas, at the shipyard gate</cite></div>

  <p>A workers' union was the only legal vehicle left to fight a party you couldn't out-vote. It grew to ten million members.</p>

  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Lech Wałęsa"></div>
  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Solidarity (Polish trade union)"></div>

  <div class="note"><b>One thing bugged me, and I said so:</b> they're gentrifying the area, dropping in modern buildings that don't fit. Leave it alone.</div>
</section>

<section class="chapter" id="oliwa">
  <div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 03 · Oliwa</div>
    <h2>Oliwa, and a place for the families</h2></div>

  <p>We took the train to Oliwa, where I'd argue the whole Tri-City is at its best — calm, close to both the business center and the beach. It was its own town once. One mayor borrowed a fortune to build a casino and out-do <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopot" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Sopot</a>, couldn't repay it, and that's how Gdańsk swallowed Oliwa whole. The park goes back to the twelfth century; I walk it most mornings.</p>

  <p>Inside the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliwa_Cathedral" target="_blank" rel="noopener">cathedral</a> the ceiling does the work — white vaults pulling your eyes up, light pouring onto a dark-and-gold altar. Jonas didn't say a word in there, which is about the highest review a building gets.</p>

  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Oliwa Cathedral"></div>

  <p>The <a href="https://www.google.com/travel/search?q=oliwa%20pensionat" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Oliwa Park Residence</a> is genuinely nice in a low-key way: a brick building with a wooden porch, baskets of yellow flowers spilling off the rails, and a ridiculous red Dodge parked on the cobbles like it owns them. The rooms are big, and the whole place feels like you've stepped outside the city — a properly restful, recreational kind of quiet.</p>

<section class="chapter" id="gdynia">
  <div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 04 · Gdynia</div>
    <h2>Gdynia, built from nothing in three years</h2></div>

  <p>North to Gdynia, where the usual assumption gets it backwards. Everyone comes to Gdańsk for the medieval stuff, but it's <em>Gdynia</em> that's protected as a heritage site — for its <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown,_Gdynia" target="_blank" rel="noopener">modernism</a>. After WWI, Gdańsk became a <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_City_of_Danzig" target="_blank" rel="noopener">"free city,"</a> German in everything that mattered, leaving Poland with no port. So they built one.</p>

  <div class="stats">
    <div class="stat"><div class="n">20</div><div class="l">houses in the village before it began</div></div>
    <div class="stat"><div class="n">3&nbsp;yrs</div><div class="l">from village to a whole modernist city</div></div>
    <div class="stat"><div class="n">1 plan</div><div class="l">every developer built to one master architect's vision</div></div>
  </div>

  <p>Land auctioned in parcels, but every developer had to build to one master architect's plan. The result looks organic and was completely planted — clean, geometric, uniform. People write it off as ugly concrete, which is a lazy read.</p>

  <div class="wiki" data-wiki="Gdynia"></div>

  <p>We had a beer at <a href="https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&amp;query=Honolulu%20Gdynia" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Honolulu</a>. And then the bit Jonas couldn't stop looking at: a bright yellow bust that reads unmistakably as Jesus, crowned with a little straw sombrero. In a country this Catholic, that takes real nerve to leave just sitting out. I loved it.</p>

<section class="chapter" id="fyrtel">
  <div class="chap-head"><div class="chap-num">Stop 05 · Fyrtel</div>
    <h2>Fyrtel, where the day found its second story</h2></div>

  <p>The last stop was Fyrtel — cables across white tile, its own gravity inside. Black matte walls, mid-century chairs, expressionist paintings everywhere.</p>

  <div class="pull">"It's a small galaxy here. More relaxed. More tranquil."</div>

  <p>We'd walked in on a hanging — a few guys setting up a show for a local painter, one up on the sofa driving a nail into bare plaster, the others holding blank canvases. Jonas got pulled in. He offered his read on placement, and they actually listened: one canvas ended up turned ninety degrees, hung in a way they hadn't planned. One piece was from the <a href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0142688/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Ninth Gate</a>, and we went back and forth on what it meant for a while. Didn't settle it. Didn't really need to.</p>

  function wIcon(){return '<svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="none" stroke="currentColor" stroke-width="1.6"><circle cx="12" cy="12" r="9"/><path d="M3.5 9h17M3.5 15h17M12 3a14 14 0 0 1 0 18M12 3a14 14 0 0 0 0 18"/></svg>';}
  async function loadWiki(el){
    const title=el.getAttribute('data-wiki');
    el.innerHTML='<div class="wtop"><div class="wmeta"><div class="wkicker">'+wIcon()+' Wikipedia</div><div class="wloading">Loading '+title+'…</div></div></div>';
    try{
      const r=await fetch('https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/'+encodeURIComponent(title.replace(/ /g,'_')));
      if(!r.ok) throw new Error(r.status);
      const d=await r.json();
      const thumb=d.thumbnail&&d.thumbnail.source;
      const url=(d.content_urls&&d.content_urls.desktop&&d.content_urls.desktop.page)||('https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'+encodeURIComponent(title));
      el.innerHTML=
        '<div class="wtop">'+
          (thumb?'<div class="wthumb"><img loading="lazy" src="'+thumb+'" alt=""></div>':'')+
          '<div class="wmeta">'+
            '<div class="wkicker">'+wIcon()+' Wikipedia</div>'+
            '<h4>'+(d.title||title)+'</h4>'+
            '<p class="wextract">'+(d.extract||'')+'</p>'+
          '</div>'+
        '</div>'+
        '<div class="wfoot"><span>'+(d.description?d.description.charAt(0).toUpperCase()+d.description.slice(1):'Reference')+'</span>'+
        '<a href="'+url+'" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read on Wikipedia ↗</a></div>';
    }catch(e){
      el.innerHTML='<div class="wtop"><div class="wmeta"><div class="wkicker">'+wIcon()+' Wikipedia</div>'+
        '<h4>'+title+'</h4><p class="wextract">Couldn\'t load the live summary. '+
        '<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'+encodeURIComponent(title.replace(/ /g,'_'))+'" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open on Wikipedia ↗</a></p></div></div>';
    }
  }

  function start(){
    initMaps();
    document.querySelectorAll('.jonas-story .wiki[data-wiki]').forEach(loadWiki);
  }
  if(document.readyState==='complete' || document.readyState==='interactive') start();
  else window.addEventListener('DOMContentLoaded', start);
})();
